Life-science expansion capital

Capital for life-science companies that already sell, repaid from the sales it creates

A long-term reserve funds medtech and pharma companies from capital-scarce markets as they expand abroad. Companies repay through a capped share of new revenue and keep their equity. Investors receive distributions that move with the reserve, not a fixed dividend.

The thesis

Five conditions for a reserve that funds companies

Pay out what the reserve can sustain

Total payouts stay at a few percent of the capital raised each year, not the 8–15% coupons on preferred stock issued by listed treasury companies.

  • At 15% crypto growth, an all-crypto reserve sustained 0.5% a year for 14 years with depletion risk within 5%.
  • The same reserve with a 40% Treasury-bill sleeve sustained 3.0%.

Hold safe assets against commitments

A sleeve of short-term government bills covers operating costs and committed milestone tranches for several years, so a market fall does not force sales at the bottom.

  • Replacing 40% of the bitcoin with Treasury bills raised the sustainable payout in every forward scenario.
  • Adding ether instead lowered depletion risk by at most 7 percentage points: digital assets tend to move together.

Let the investor return flex

Distributions vary with the reserve's value and can be reduced or deferred, so investors share losses instead of passing them to the companies being funded.

  • With no fixed dividend, linking the funding budget to the reserve cut depletion risk from 27% to 6%.
  • With a fixed 8% dividend, the same rule cut it only from 54% to 49%.

Bitcoin 60 / Treasury bills 40, 15% crypto growth, 14 years.

Follow the reserve, keep promises

The funding budget follows the reserve's smoothed value, but tranches already promised to companies that met their milestones are ring-fenced, so no company is stranded mid-programme.

  • Modelled as the trailing 12-month average value of the reserve, capped at twice the base budget.

Check every repayment

A revenue share only works when sales can be verified, through audited accounts or payment records.

  • South African merchants whose revenue-based advances were priced to repay 1.3 times had repaid 1.06 times after a year.
  • Life-science companies with audited product sales are easier to monitor than small merchants.

Source: Russel, Shi and Clarke, South African Reserve Bank, 2025.

How it works

Five steps from capital to repayment

This is the design the stress test supports. Final terms will be set out in the offering documents and may differ.

Raise capital once

Capital is raised once through a security token offering, in a jurisdiction that regulates security tokens. Investors hold a tokenized security, not a utility or governance token.

  • Offered only to investors who are eligible under the applicable securities rules.
  • Size, price and terms are set out in the offering documents.

In the stress test

All amounts per 100 of capital raised; 90 enters the reserve after offering and set-up costs.

Hold a mixed reserve

The reserve holds digital assets alongside a sleeve of short-term government bills, in segregated, insured custody.

  • The safe-asset sleeve covers operating costs and committed tranches for several years.
  • Holdings are reported through third-party attestations and on-chain proof of reserves, alongside audited financial statements.

In the stress test

Bitcoin 60 / Treasury bills 40, rebalanced monthly.

Fund expansion against milestones

Companies with approved or marketed products receive capital to sell abroad. Each tranche is released once an independent committee verifies a commercial milestone.

  • Milestones such as a marketing authorization in a new country, a signed distribution agreement or a revenue threshold.
  • Funds go to approvals abroad, market access, distribution, sales capacity and working capital.

In the stress test

An expansion budget of 10% of capital raised a year, deployed over seven years.

Collect a share of new revenue

Each company pays a share of the sales the expansion brings in until it has repaid a capped multiple of what it received. No equity changes hands.

  • Repayments start once the expansion is selling: sooner for products already authorized in the target market.
  • Sales are verified from audited accounts or payment records.

In the stress test

Repayment starts 12 or 24 months after each tranche and stops at 1.5 times the tranche (also tested at 2 times).

Distribute flexibly

Distributions to token holders follow a rule linked to the reserve's value. They can fall, be deferred or be skipped. Operating costs and committed company tranches are paid first.

  • No fixed dividend and no guaranteed return.
  • The distribution rule is set out in the offering documents.

In the stress test

Operating costs of 2% of capital raised a year. The design that kept its capital paid no dividend.

For companies

Funding for companies that already sell

Built for medtech and pharma companies with approved or marketed products and audited sales, expanding into a named foreign market. Early-stage research before approval is not the target: it rarely earns revenue in time to repay.

  1. Check the fit

    An approved or marketed product, audited sales and an expansion plan for a named market.

  2. Agree milestones

    For example, a signed distribution agreement and a first revenue threshold.

  3. Receive tranches

    Capital is released as an independent committee verifies each milestone.

  4. Repay from new sales

    A share of the new revenue, starting about 12 months after each tranche.

  5. Stop at the cap

    Repayment ends once a capped multiple of the tranche is reached.

  1. Check the fit

    An approved or marketed product at home, audited sales and an expansion plan for a named market.

  2. Agree milestones

    Starting with the marketing authorization in the target country, then distribution and revenue.

  3. Receive tranches

    Capital is released as an independent committee verifies each milestone.

  4. Repay from new sales

    A share of the new revenue, starting about 24 months after each tranche to allow for certification and market access.

  5. Stop at the cap

    Repayment ends once a capped multiple of the tranche is reached.

Start dates and the cap are the values used in the stress test (repayment capped at 1.5 times each tranche). Actual terms are agreed with each company.

Regulation and protections

A regulated security, with the reserve in view

Issued under securities law

  • In the EU, a token with the economic rights of preferred stock is likely to be treated as a financial instrument, outside the crypto-asset regulation and subject to prospectus and trading rules.
  • In the United States, a tokenized security must be registered or exempt, whatever its format.
  • The rules that apply depend on where the offering is made, and only eligible investors can take part.

Sources: European Securities and Markets Authority, 2024; US Securities and Exchange Commission, 2026.

Segregated, insured, reported

  • Reserve assets held in segregated, insured custody.
  • Third-party attestations and on-chain proof of reserves show holdings at a point in time.
  • Proof-of-reserve reports are not audits, so they supplement audited financial statements rather than replace them.

Source: Public Company Accounting Oversight Board, 2024.

Independent checks on capital and conduct

  • A majority of independent directors with fiduciary duties to token holders, as proposed.
  • An independent committee verifies each milestone before a tranche is released.
  • No equity in funded companies; repayment through a capped revenue share.

Rules in the countries we fund

  • The International Monetary Fund warns that tokenized cross-border capital can speed outflows and currency substitution, so host-country regulators may limit it.
  • A dollar-linked reserve financing companies that earn local currencies creates a currency mismatch; revenue earned in export markets can reduce it.

Sources: Adrian, International Monetary Fund, 2026; Eichengreen, Hausmann and Panizza, 2023.

  • The probability that the reserve runs out under stated return assumptions, rather than projected returns.

Risks

Risks you should weigh

You could lose some or all of your investment. These are the main risks; the offering documents will set out the full list.

  • The reserve can run out

    Even the recommended design ran out within 14 years in some simulated paths: 6% at 15% crypto growth and 19% at 0%, with no dividend and a budget linked to the reserve.

  • Digital assets are volatile

    Bitcoin fell by more than half from a previous peak four times since 2017, by up to 84%. Digital assets move together, so holding several protects little.

  • No guaranteed return

    Distributions can be reduced, deferred or skipped. There is no fixed dividend.

  • Repayments can fall short

    Expansions can fail, and revenue shares can underpay: advances elsewhere priced to repay 1.3 times had repaid 1.06 times after a year.

  • The token may be hard to sell

    Secondary trading may be limited, and the token may trade below the value of the reserve. In September 2026, only 4 of the 20 largest listed treasury companies traded above the value of their holdings.

  • Rules can change

    Regulation of tokenized securities and of cross-border capital is still evolving and may restrict the offering or the companies it funds.

  • Currency mismatch

    Companies earning local currencies repay a reserve held in dollar-linked assets; exchange-rate moves can reduce what they can pay.

  • The model has limits

    The stress test resamples ten years of returns and cannot produce conditions outside that history. It leaves out trading, custody and tax costs. It was written by Agile Dynamics staff, including the author of the original proposal.

Questions

Questions, answered

Show questions for
Is there a fixed dividend?

No. In the stress test, a fixed 8% dividend emptied the reserve within 14 years in 54–60% of simulated paths at 15% crypto growth. Distributions instead follow a rule linked to the reserve's value and can be reduced, deferred or skipped.

What return can I expect?

We don't project one. Distributions depend on the reserve's value and on repayments from companies, and both are uncertain. The offering documents will set out the distribution rule.

Who can invest?

Only investors who are eligible under the securities rules of the jurisdiction where the offering is made, and only through the offering documents. This page is not an offer.

Can I sell my tokens?

The structure is designed for trading on regulated secondary venues, but there may be few buyers, and the price may be below the value of the reserve.

Do you take equity?

No. Companies repay a share of the revenue the expansion brings in, until they have repaid a capped multiple of the capital they received.

Which companies fit?

Medtech and pharma companies with approved or marketed products, audited sales and a plan to enter a named foreign market, especially from economies with shallow capital markets.

When do repayments start?

Once the expansion is selling. The stress test assumed about 12 months after each tranche for products already authorized in the target market, and about 24 months for those that need authorization first.

Why hold digital assets at all?

A reserve of Treasury bills alone can fund 7% a year for 14 years, but only by spending itself down; it kept its value only at payouts of 2%. Digital assets give a chance of keeping the capital intact at higher payouts, at the price of a real risk of running out. In this design the revenue share, not the digital assets, does most of the work.

Why not fund early-stage drug research?

Clinical development of recently approved drugs took a median of 8.3 years, and only 13.8% of programmes entering human trials reach approval. A reserve that must pay out for that long can release little, while companies that already sell can pay it back.

Who did the research?

Henrik von Scheel, Nikola Mandic and Paul Lalovich of Agile Dynamics. Agile Dynamics proposed the structure, and Paul Lalovich wrote the original proposal. You can request the paper and its code from the authors.
